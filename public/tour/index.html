Part 1 The game, step by step

Every picture below is a real frame from the running game, captured in a headless browser.

1 Open the page

First visit shows a short "How to play". After that the home screen runs a live bot match behind the menu, kept cheap on purpose (12 fps, paused when the window loses focus). Settings cover bot level, rounds, arena, theme, power-ups, sudden death, 2 vs 2 teams, colour-blind colours, vibration and reduced motion.

2 Pick a look

The same simulation drives three renderers. Classic is pixel art on a 2D canvas. 3D uses Three.js with shadows and bloom. DLSS 5 is a parody: over-sharpened, over-saturated, glossy "Neural Faces", a fake frame-generation counter and a real dynamic resolution.

3 Arenas and themes

Five layouts and five themes, chosen per match or at random per round. Themes are purely visual and work in all three renderers.

4 Power-ups

Bricks hide eight kinds of items. The board below shows all of them, plus a shield bubble (top-left player), a remote bomb that pulses red, a sliding bomb after a kick, and a curse halo.

  • Bomb +1 bomb at once
  • Range longer blasts
  • Speed run faster
  • Kick walk into a bomb to slide it
  • Remote press bomb again to detonate
  • Pass walk through bombs
  • Shield 5 seconds of flame immunity
  • Skull random curse: slow, fast, reversed, no bombs, auto bombs

5 Teams

With four players the match can be 2 vs 2, with optional friendly fire. Team rings and HUD groups ("Gold" and "Cyan") show who is on whose side, and the round goes to the last team standing.

6 Play online

The host creates a room and shares a link or QR code. Guests need no account. Names are sanitised, the host decides on rematches, and a guest who drops reconnects within 30 seconds while a bot keeps the slot warm. The server is a small relay that enforces roles, message types, rates and connection limits.

7 On a phone

Virtual joystick and bomb button on touch screens, optional vibration on the phone and on gamepads, an install button, and a lighter 3D path (lower resolution, fewer lights) for weaker GPUs.

Part 2 Story mode

Written in the spirit of arcade fighting games: ancient grudges, family ties and a final boss who is somebody's grandfather.

Twenty years ago Baron von Boom stole the Eternal Fuse, the artifact that powers every bomb. Tonight's tournament decides who gets it back. Also, grandpa is up and about.

Mama's ending
  1. Mama Kaboom raises her rolling pin. The arena rises to its feet.
  2. 'I told you the Baron was a bad influence.' Twenty years of 'I told you so' pay out at once.
  3. The Eternal Fuse goes home and into the cupboard, behind the good plates.
  4. Ignatius and Baron are grounded. They are 58 and 60-something. They accept this.
  5. Mama's last words to the crowd: 'Did everybody eat? Good. Now go home.'
Dolores's ending
  1. Dolores walks off with the prize money, the Eternal Fuse and the best photo of the night.
  2. She ends her partnership with the Baron. He asks about the shared yacht. She sinks it.
  3. Ignatius sends flowers. Dynamita sends a thank-you note. Mama sends a rolling pin.
  4. Tony and Kapow open a restaurant with her winnings. It is called Auntie's. It is very loud.
  5. Dolores lights a cigarette with the Eternal Fuse. The fire marshal has questions.
Tony's ending
  1. Tony wins the tournament. He wipes a tear off his watch.
  2. The Baron fires him over the phone. Tony is thrilled: his first day off since 2009.
  3. Kapow gets Tony's autograph, then asks for a second one, then a hat.
  4. Aunt Dolores wants a cut. Cousin Dynamita wants a rematch. Tony wants a nap.
  5. He is on time for the ceremony at last. Nobody else is. Typical.
Fizzle's ending
  1. Sir Fizzle takes off his bucket helmet. The crowd gasps. He has a really nice face.
  2. The Baron begs for mercy. 'You fizzled me first,' says Fizzle, and nobody gets the joke.
  3. Dynamita texts her congratulations. He reads it nine times and walks into a door.
  4. He carries the Eternal Fuse home on his horse. The horse is not impressed.
  5. Knight, champion and, for one night, a man who did not fizzle.

Guest browserspredict their own player, interpolate the rest ~100 ms behind
  • One simulation. sim.js is pure and deterministic. Solo, local, host and guest prediction all run the same code, covered by 75 tests including a host plus guest harness with simulated latency, jitter and packet loss.
  • Bots that think ahead. Danger is a time map that includes chain reactions; they flee on a clock, take bonuses, avoid sudden death, and come in three levels.
  • Frugal by design. No build step, a service worker that is network-first with an offline fallback, a home-screen demo throttled to a few frames per second, and no work at all while paused.
  • Hardened relay. First subscriber owns the room, message types are allow-listed per role, traffic is rate-limited, and every refusal is logged in a format the host monitors.
  • Original art. Sprites and portraits are drawn in code or generated and converted to pixel art; no third-party game assets. A fan-made tribute, not affiliated with Konami.
